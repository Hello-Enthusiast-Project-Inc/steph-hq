6–8 months

Week 1 — Face-to-Face & First Impressions
Mon — Face-to-face connection
Read Aloud: Black & White by Tana Hoban — Hold the book 20–30cm from Hayes. Slowly turn pages and pause on each bold pattern, letting him study the contrast.
Motor/Sensory Activity: Tummy time on a firm surface for 2–3 minutes. Get down at Hayes's level and smile at him so he lifts his head to see your face.
Song/Poem: Twinkle Twinkle Little Star — Sing slowly while making eye contact. Gently tap his hands together on the beat.
Woven Ideas: While changing Hayes's diaper, narrate what you see: "I see your toes! One, two, three..." This builds language even in routine moments.
Tue — High contrast exploration
Read Aloud: Baby Faces by DK — Point to each baby's face and name the emotion: "This baby is happy! Look at that smile." Pause and let Hayes study each photo.
Motor/Sensory Activity: Place a high-contrast card (black and white stripes or bullseye) propped up during tummy time. Let Hayes lift his head and look at it for 2–3 minutes.
Song/Poem: You Are My Sunshine — Sing while gently stroking Hayes's cheeks and arms. Slow the tempo to keep it soothing.
Woven Ideas: At bathtime, gently splash the water and say "splash!" each time. Repetition of cause and effect starts here.
Wed — Gentle sensory awakening
Read Aloud: Pat the Bunny by Dorothy Kunhardt — Guide Hayes's hand to touch the bunny's fur and the scratchy face. Say "soft!" and "scratchy!" with animated expressions.
Motor/Sensory Activity: Lay Hayes on his back and gently bicycle his legs, singing a slow rhythm. Do 5–6 gentle cycles, then pause and smile.
Song/Poem: Round and Round the Garden — Trace circles on Hayes's palm, then "walk" your fingers up his arm. Pause before the tickle so he learns to anticipate.
Woven Ideas: Hold Hayes facing outward at a window. Describe what you see outside: "I see a tree! The leaves are green." Fresh scenery feeds curiosity.
Thu — Babbling back-and-forth
Read Aloud: Global Babies by The Global Fund for Children — Show each page and say "Hello, baby!" in a warm voice. Pause after each page and wait to see if Hayes coos or babbles back.
Motor/Sensory Activity: Tummy time with a rolled-up towel under Hayes's chest for extra support. Place your face at his level and make silly sounds to encourage him to lift and hold his head.
Song/Poem: Rock-a-Bye Baby — Rock Hayes gently in your arms while singing. Sway side to side in a slow, predictable rhythm.
Woven Ideas: During a feed, pause and say "Mmm, yummy!" then wait. If Hayes makes a sound, respond enthusiastically: "You think so too!" This models turn-taking.
Fri — Playful connection & review
Read Aloud: Peek-a-Boo! by Roberta Grobel Intrater — Cover your face with the book, then reveal with a big "Peek-a-boo!" Watch for Hayes's reaction and repeat if he smiles.
Motor/Sensory Activity: Hold Hayes in a supported standing position on your lap for 30 seconds, letting him bear a little weight on his legs. Bounce gently and cheer him on.
Song/Poem: If You're Happy and You Know It — Clap Hayes's hands together gently on "clap your hands." Use an excited voice for each verse.
Woven Ideas: Blow gentle raspberries on Hayes's belly during a diaper change. Pause and see if he tries to vocalize or laugh in response. Celebrate any sounds he makes.
Week 2 — Mirror Play & Babbling
Mon — Mirror discovery
Read Aloud: Baby Faces by DK — Hold the book next to a small mirror. Point to a smiling baby in the book, then point to Hayes's reflection: "That's you! You're smiling too!"
Motor/Sensory Activity: Tummy time in front of a baby-safe mirror propped on the floor. Tap the mirror and say Hayes's name so he lifts his head to look. Aim for 3 minutes.
Song/Poem: Twinkle Twinkle Little Star — Sing in front of the mirror so Hayes can see both your face and his own. Point to his reflection on the word "star."
Woven Ideas: Carry Hayes around the house and stop at different mirrors. Say "Who's that? It's Hayes!" each time. Familiarity with his own reflection supports self-awareness.
Tue — Extended tummy time
Read Aloud: Hello, World! by Jill McDonald — Read with bright enthusiasm, pointing out colors on each page: "Look, the sun is yellow!" Let Hayes touch the pages if he reaches.
Motor/Sensory Activity: Tummy time for 4–5 minutes. Place a crinkle toy just within reach in front of Hayes. Encourage him by patting the toy so it makes noise.
Song/Poem: Row Row Row Your Boat — Hold Hayes's hands and gently rock him forward and back in a seated position on your lap as you sing.
Woven Ideas: Crinkle a piece of tissue paper near Hayes's ear (not too close). Watch his reaction, then let him grab it with supervision. New textures and sounds spark neural connections.
Wed — Babbling dialogue
Read Aloud: Goodnight Moon by Margaret Wise Brown — Read in a hushed, rhythmic voice. Pause at the end of each "Goodnight..." line and wait for Hayes to babble or coo before continuing.
Motor/Sensory Activity: Place Hayes on his back. Hold a soft rattle above his chest and shake it gently. When he reaches up, let him grab it. Repeat 3–4 times to encourage arm reaching.
Song/Poem: Baa Baa Black Sheep — Sing with exaggerated mouth movements so Hayes can see how your lips form each sound. Pause after "Yes sir, yes sir" to invite a babble.
Woven Ideas: During a quiet moment, imitate any sound Hayes makes. If he says "ahh," you say "ahh" right back. Wait. If he does it again, copy again. This is the foundation of conversation.
Thu — Sensory textures
Read Aloud: Baby Touch and Feel: Animals by DK — Guide Hayes's fingers over each textured patch. Name the animal and the feeling: "The kitten is soft. The lizard is bumpy!"
Motor/Sensory Activity: Tummy time with a play mat. Place two different textured cloths (smooth satin, soft fleece) in front of Hayes so he can reach and feel them while propping up on his forearms.
Song/Poem: The Itsy Bitsy Spider — Walk your fingers up Hayes's arm for the spider climbing. Wiggle your fingers down for the rain. Use big facial expressions throughout.
Woven Ideas: Let Hayes hold a cool metal spoon and a warm soft cloth (warmed briefly in your hands). Say "cool!" and "warm!" as he explores each one. Temperature variety builds sensory vocabulary.
Fri — Social smiling
Read Aloud: I Love You Through and Through by Bernadette Rossetti-Shustak — Read with warm affection. Touch Hayes's toes when the book mentions toes, his hair when it mentions hair. Make it physical and connected.
Motor/Sensory Activity: Sit Hayes on your lap facing you, supporting him at the hips. Make funny faces — stick out your tongue, puff your cheeks, open your mouth wide. See if he tries to copy any expression.
Song/Poem: You Are My Sunshine — Sing verse one while holding Hayes close, then hold him at arm's length and beam a big smile at him. Alternate close and far throughout the song.
Woven Ideas: Play a gentle game where you hide behind a burp cloth and pop out with "Boo!" Keep the cloth small so Hayes can still see your outline. Early disappearance-and-return games build trust.
Week 3 — Reaching & Rolling
Mon — Reaching for a dangling toy
Read Aloud: The Very Hungry Caterpillar by Eric Carle — Slowly move your finger through each hole in the pages. Name each food with relish: "One apple — crunch!" Encourage Hayes to touch the pages.
Motor/Sensory Activity: Hang a lightweight toy (rattle or fabric ring) from your hand about 15cm above Hayes while he lies on his back. Wiggle it gently and encourage him to swat and grab at it.
Song/Poem: Twinkle Twinkle Little Star — Hold a small star-shaped toy or cut-out while singing. Move it slowly in a gentle arc and let Hayes reach for it during the song.
Woven Ideas: Place a toy slightly to Hayes's side during tummy time — not directly ahead but at a 45-degree angle. This nudges him to shift his weight, a precursor to rolling.
Tue — Rolling practice
Read Aloud: Where Is Baby's Belly Button? by Karen Katz — Lift each flap with suspense: "Where are baby's eyes?" Pause, then reveal. Let Hayes help lift flaps if he reaches for them.
Motor/Sensory Activity: Place Hayes on his back and hold a toy to one side, near his hip. Gently guide him to roll from back to side by bending his top knee across. Cheer when he gets to his side. Try both directions.
Song/Poem: Round and Round the Garden — After the finger play on his palm, try it on his tummy. The anticipation of the tickle will make him squirm and engage his core.
Woven Ideas: When Hayes drops a toy, say "Uh-oh! It fell down!" and pick it up. Repeat. Naming what happens builds language around cause and effect, and he may start dropping things on purpose soon.
Wed — Voice localization
Read Aloud: Global Babies by The Global Fund for Children — Read from slightly to one side of Hayes, then shift to the other side. Notice if he turns toward your voice. Say "I'm over here!" when you move.
Motor/Sensory Activity: During tummy time, call Hayes's name from different positions — left side, right side, slightly behind. Encourage him to push up and turn his head toward your voice. Keep sessions to 3–4 minutes.
Song/Poem: Rock-a-Bye Baby — Sing from across the room, then move closer. Vary your distance so Hayes practices locating where the sound comes from.
Woven Ideas: Shake a small bell or rattle behind a cloth. When Hayes looks curious, reveal it and say "You found it!" Associating sound with source is a key cognitive step.
Thu — Grasping practice
Read Aloud: Pat the Bunny by Dorothy Kunhardt — This time, let Hayes take the lead. Place the book in front of him and let him pat, grab, and mouth it. Narrate what he's doing: "You're touching the bunny!"
Motor/Sensory Activity: Offer Hayes a soft fabric ring. When he grabs it, gently tug — just enough so he feels resistance and grips tighter. Release and celebrate: "Strong grip!" Repeat with each hand.
Song/Poem: If You're Happy and You Know It — Place a rattle in Hayes's hand and shake his hand gently on "clap your hands." Switch to stomping his feet on the next verse.
Woven Ideas: Place a few safe kitchen items on a towel in front of Hayes — a wooden spoon, a silicone spatula, a small whisk. Let him grab and mouth each one. Everyday objects are the best toys at this age.
Fri — Supported mini-sits
Read Aloud: Ten Little Fingers and Ten Little Toes by Mem Fox — Touch Hayes's fingers and toes as you read. Count along slowly. The rhyming pattern is soothing and predictable — pause before the rhyming word to build anticipation.
Motor/Sensory Activity: Sit Hayes in a Boppy pillow or between your legs on the floor. Place a toy in front of him and let him lean forward to reach it. Support him as needed, letting him practice balancing for 1–2 minutes.
Song/Poem: Baa Baa Black Sheep — Bounce Hayes gently on your knee to the beat. Emphasize the "baa baa" by nodding your head dramatically so he can see the rhythm.
Woven Ideas: Hold Hayes in front of a running faucet (test temperature first). Let the water trickle over his fingers. Say "water! Splashy splash!" New sensory experiences in safe contexts build confidence.
Week 4 — Sitting & Cause-and-Effect
Mon — Supported sitting exploration
Read Aloud: Black & White by Tana Hoban — Prop Hayes in a supported sit and hold the book in front of him. Now that he's more upright, the perspective changes. Let him reach out and bat the pages.
Motor/Sensory Activity: Sit Hayes between your legs on the floor with toys scattered in a semicircle around him. Support his trunk lightly and let him lean to reach different toys. This builds core strength and balance.
Song/Poem: Row Row Row Your Boat — Sit facing Hayes, hold his hands, and gently pull him forward and lean him back with each "row." This is a mini sit-up workout disguised as a song!
Woven Ideas: Fill a small container with dried pasta (large shells, supervised). Let Hayes reach in and grab handfuls. The sound, texture, and weight are all novel. Narrate: "Scoopy scoop! You got some!"
Tue — Cause-and-effect discovery
Read Aloud: Peek-a-Boo! by Roberta Grobel Intrater — Let Hayes hold one side of the book while you hold the other. When he accidentally closes it, say "Where did the baby go?" and reopen it together.
Motor/Sensory Activity: Place a toy on top of a small cloth in front of Hayes. Show him that pulling the cloth brings the toy closer. Guide his hand the first time, then see if he tries independently.
Song/Poem: The Itsy Bitsy Spider — Use a popup toy or jack-in-the-box alongside the song. Pop it up at "out came the sun." The surprise reinforces cause and effect.
Woven Ideas: Tape a sheet of bubble wrap to the high chair tray (edges folded under for safety). Let Hayes bang and press it. Every pop is instant feedback — cause and effect in action.
Wed — Turn-taking vocalizations
Read Aloud: Goodnight Moon by Margaret Wise Brown — Whisper the goodnight lines and pause. If Hayes makes any sound, respond with "Goodnight, Hayes." Treat every babble as a real word in the conversation.
Motor/Sensory Activity: Place a ball in front of Hayes during supported sitting. Roll it gently toward him and say "Your turn!" If he bats it, say "You rolled it! My turn!" Roll it back. Early turn-taking through play.
Song/Poem: You Are My Sunshine — Sing the first line, then stop and wait. If Hayes coos or babbles, say "Beautiful singing!" and continue. Stop-and-wait builds anticipation and encourages vocalization.
Woven Ideas: During meal prep, hold Hayes on your hip and narrate: "I'm cutting a banana — chop chop chop! It's yellow and soft." Everyday narration is the single most powerful language builder.
Thu — Pressing and pushing
Read Aloud: Baby Touch and Feel: Animals by DK — This time focus on the sounds: "The cow says moo! The duck says quack!" Use big pauses between animals and watch if Hayes babbles his own "animal sounds" in response.
Motor/Sensory Activity: Offer Hayes a toy with a big button that lights up or makes noise when pressed. Guide his hand to push it the first time. Watch his face when it activates — then let him figure it out alone.
Song/Poem: Twinkle Twinkle Little Star — Use a light-up toy that activates with a press. Help Hayes press it at key moments in the song: "like a diamond in the sky" — press! Light!
Woven Ideas: Blow up a balloon (keep it in your hands at all times for safety). Let Hayes tap it. It moves in unpredictable ways — fascinating for a baby learning that their actions cause reactions.
Fri — Interactive play consolidation
Read Aloud: Hello, World! by Jill McDonald — Read with Hayes sitting on your lap facing the book. Let him turn pages (with help). Celebrate each turn: "You turned the page! What's next?"
Motor/Sensory Activity: Create a mini obstacle: a rolled towel for Hayes to lean over from a sitting position to grab a toy on the other side. Support him as he stretches, building core and confidence.
Song/Poem: Round and Round the Garden — Do the finger play on Hayes's belly, then try it on the sole of his foot. Different body parts keep the same game feeling brand new.
Woven Ideas: Play "So Big!" — ask "How big is Hayes?" and stretch his arms up: "Sooooo big!" Repeat several times. He may start raising his arms on his own by the end of the week.
Week 5 — Reaching While Sitting & Imitation
Mon — Reaching while sitting
Read Aloud: Where Is Baby's Belly Button? by Karen Katz — Sit Hayes up and hold the book slightly to one side so he has to twist his trunk to see the flaps. Alternate sides with each page.
Motor/Sensory Activity: Sit Hayes with support and hold toys at different heights — one at chest level, one higher near his head, one to the side. Encourage him to reach in all directions while maintaining his balance.
Song/Poem: If You're Happy and You Know It — During "clap your hands," hold your hands up high so Hayes has to reach up to clap with you. This challenges his sitting balance while he moves his arms.
Woven Ideas: Fill a small zip-lock bag with hair gel and a few drops of food coloring (seal it tight with tape). Let Hayes squish and press it on his high chair tray. Mess-free sensory play!
Tue — Banging objects together
Read Aloud: The Very Hungry Caterpillar by Eric Carle — Give Hayes a small wooden block to hold while you read. When the caterpillar eats, encourage Hayes to bang the block on the table: "Munch munch munch!"
Motor/Sensory Activity: Give Hayes two small wooden blocks (or stacking cups). Show him how to bang them together — clap them and make a big "BANG!" sound. Hand them over and cheer any attempt to imitate.
Song/Poem: Baa Baa Black Sheep — Give Hayes a wooden spoon and a pot lid. Bang the "drum" on the beat of the song. Let him take over the drumming while you sing.
Woven Ideas: Let Hayes bang a spoon on different surfaces: the high chair tray, a book, a cushion. Name each sound: "That's a loud bang! That's a soft thud!" He's learning physics through play.
Wed — Imitating mouth movements
Read Aloud: Baby Faces by DK — Focus on the mouths in each photo. Open your mouth wide and say "ahhh," then purse your lips and say "ooo." Watch if Hayes tries to copy your mouth shapes.
Motor/Sensory Activity: Sit face-to-face with Hayes. Stick out your tongue slowly — hold it — then pull it back in. Repeat 3–4 times. Try big open mouth, puffed cheeks, and lip smacking. Celebrate any attempt to imitate.
Song/Poem: Row Row Row Your Boat — Exaggerate your mouth on each word. "Roooow" with a big round mouth, "merrily" with a wide smile. Sing slowly enough that Hayes can study your face.
Woven Ideas: Blow raspberries on Hayes's arm, then on your own arm so he can see and hear. Say "Your turn!" and wait. Many babies start imitating raspberries this week — it builds oral motor skills for speech.
Thu — Bilateral hand play
Read Aloud: I Love You Through and Through by Bernadette Rossetti-Shustak — Clap Hayes's hands together at the end of each line. By mid-book, pause and see if he claps on his own to continue the pattern.
Motor/Sensory Activity: Give Hayes a small soft ball. Show him how to pass it from one hand to the other. Gently guide the transfer if needed. Say "This hand... now THIS hand!" Left-right-left.
Song/Poem: Twinkle Twinkle Little Star — Hold a scarf in front of Hayes with both your hands. Slowly lift it up on "up above the world so high." Drop it on his lap. Let him grab it with both hands and lift it himself.
Woven Ideas: Offer Hayes a piece of banana or soft pear to hold (if eating solids). Holding food and bringing it to his own mouth is fantastic bilateral coordination and builds independence at mealtimes.
Fri — Vocal imitation games
Read Aloud: Ten Little Fingers and Ten Little Toes by Mem Fox — Read with animated pitch changes. Go high on "fingers" and low on "toes." Wiggle Hayes's fingers and toes in rhythm with the words.
Motor/Sensory Activity: Place Hayes in supported sitting with a scarf draped over a toy in front of him. Say "Where did it go?" and encourage him to pull the scarf off with both hands to reveal the toy. Repeat with different toys.
Song/Poem: The Itsy Bitsy Spider — Slow the song way down. Emphasize each syllable. After "itsy bitsy," pause and wait. See if Hayes makes a sound to urge you to continue. Any sound counts!
Woven Ideas: Record Hayes's babbling on your phone and play it back to him. Watch his face — he may recognize his own voice! This delightful moment builds self-awareness and interest in sounds.
Week 6 — Pushing Up & Mouthing
Mon — Pushing up on hands
Read Aloud: Pat the Bunny by Dorothy Kunhardt — Place the book flat on the floor during tummy time. Hayes can look down at it while pushing up on his hands. Turn pages for him and keep the interaction going.
Motor/Sensory Activity: During tummy time, place your hands flat on the floor in front of Hayes so he can see them. Push up and say "Up!" Encourage him to straighten his arms and lift his chest fully off the floor. Aim for 4–5 good push-ups.
Song/Poem: You Are My Sunshine — Sing during tummy time to keep Hayes motivated. Get low on the floor so he can see your face. Your presence is the best incentive to push up and hold.
Woven Ideas: Place a few drops of vanilla extract on a cotton ball (don't let Hayes touch it directly). Waft it near his nose and say "Mmm, vanilla! Smells sweet!" Introducing varied smells builds sensory pathways.
Tue — Grasping and mouthing
Read Aloud: Baby Touch and Feel: Animals by DK — Let Hayes hold the book himself. He will likely mouth the corners — that's fine! Board books are designed for this. Narrate: "You're tasting the book! Silly Hayes!"
Motor/Sensory Activity: Offer Hayes 3–4 safe items of different textures: a rubber teether, a wooden ring, a crinkle cloth, a silicone spoon. Let him pick up each one, mouth it, drop it, and choose another. No rush — this is exploration.
Song/Poem: Rock-a-Bye Baby — Hold Hayes in your arms and gently sway while he holds a teething ring. The comfort of the song plus the oral input from the teether is a perfect soothing combo.
Woven Ideas: Freeze a damp washcloth and let Hayes gnaw on it (supervise closely). Say "Cold! Brrr!" The temperature and texture provide intense sensory input that most babies love, especially if teething.
Wed — Core strength on tummy
Read Aloud: Global Babies by The Global Fund for Children — Hold the book upright on the floor during tummy time, a few inches in front of Hayes. He'll need to push up high to see the photos. Turn pages slowly.
Motor/Sensory Activity: Place a toy just slightly out of reach during tummy time. Encourage Hayes to stretch forward on his hands to grab it. If he scoots or army-crawls even a tiny bit, celebrate enormously.
Song/Poem: Round and Round the Garden — Do the finger play on Hayes's back while he's on his tummy. The tickle at the end will make him squirm and push up — sneaky core workout!
Woven Ideas: Let Hayes play with a wooden spoon dipped in a tiny bit of yogurt (if eating solids). He can mouth it, wave it, bang it. Multi-sensory play with a simple prop.
Thu — Social laughter
Read Aloud: Peek-a-Boo! by Roberta Grobel Intrater — Build anticipation before each reveal by saying "Wheeeere's the baby..." in a rising voice. Pause longer each time. Hayes may start giggling before you even turn the page.
Motor/Sensory Activity: Play airplane: lie on your back with knees bent, place Hayes on your shins (hold him securely at the sides), and gently bounce and sway. Make airplane sounds. This is a laugher guaranteed.
Song/Poem: If You're Happy and You Know It — Add a silly verse: "If you're happy and you know it, boop your nose!" and gently boop Hayes's nose. Surprise variations get the biggest laughs.
Woven Ideas: Play the "gonna get you" game: slowly walk your fingers toward Hayes, saying "I'm gonna get youuuu..." then tickle gently. The slow approach builds delicious anticipation and social laughter.
Fri — Reaching from push-up position
Read Aloud: Black & White by Tana Hoban — Hold the book upright and slowly move it in a gentle arc left and right while Hayes is on his tummy in a push-up position. He'll shift weight to reach toward it.
Motor/Sensory Activity: During tummy time push-up, place a toy to Hayes's right. When he reaches for it with his right hand (bearing weight on the left), move it to the left side. This encourages weight shifting and builds shoulder stability.
Song/Poem: Baa Baa Black Sheep — Sing while Hayes is on his tummy. Tap the floor in front of him to the beat, encouraging him to lift one hand and tap along. Even an attempt counts!
Woven Ideas: Take Hayes outside on a blanket for tummy time. The grass, breeze, and outdoor sounds are a completely different sensory environment. Narrate everything: "Feel the wind? The grass is tickly!"
Week 7 — Pivoting & Transferring
Mon — Pivoting on tummy
Read Aloud: Goodnight Moon by Margaret Wise Brown — Place the book to one side during tummy time. Read a page, then move the book to the other side for the next page. Hayes will pivot to follow the story.
Motor/Sensory Activity: Place toys in a circle around Hayes while he's on his tummy. Encourage him to pivot (spin on his belly) to reach each toy in turn. Cheer each rotation: "You turned! There's the rattle!"
Song/Poem: Twinkle Twinkle Little Star — Sit at different positions around Hayes during tummy time and sing a line from each spot. He'll pivot to find you — a fun game of musical hide-and-seek.
Woven Ideas: Place a few ice cubes on Hayes's high chair tray. He'll try to grab them and they'll slide away — hilarious and great for fine motor persistence. Narrate: "Slippery! Try again!"
Tue — Transferring objects hand to hand
Read Aloud: Hello, World! by Jill McDonald — Give Hayes a small block to hold in one hand while you read. Periodically point to something on the page so he's tempted to reach with his block-holding hand, prompting a transfer.
Motor/Sensory Activity: Hand Hayes a small toy in his right hand. Then offer a second toy to that same hand. Watch — he should transfer the first toy to his left hand to accept the new one. If not, gently guide him. Repeat several times.
Song/Poem: Row Row Row Your Boat — Give Hayes two small shakers (or plastic eggs with rice inside, sealed). Sing and shake. He'll naturally transfer between hands as he experiments with making sounds.
Woven Ideas: Play "Which hand?" — show Hayes a toy, then hide it in one hand behind your back. Present both closed fists and say "Which hand has it?" Open whichever he reaches for. This is early problem-solving.
Wed — Anticipation games
Read Aloud: Where Is Baby's Belly Button? by Karen Katz — By now Hayes may reach for the flaps before you even ask the question. Let him lead — wait for his hand to move before you lift the flap together. He's anticipating!
Motor/Sensory Activity: Play "Ready, set, GO!" with a ball. Say "ready... set..." (long pause) "GO!" and roll the ball. After a few rounds, Hayes will start to tense up with excitement at "set," anticipating the GO.
Song/Poem: The Itsy Bitsy Spider — Pause dramatically before "down came the rain" and wiggle your fingers. Hayes may start laughing or bouncing before you even get to the rain part — he knows what's coming!
Woven Ideas: Build a simple 3-block tower and let Hayes knock it down. Say "Ready? One... two... three... CRASH!" Rebuild and repeat. The anticipation of the crash is the entire point and he'll love the power of causing it.
Thu — Two-handed exploration
Read Aloud: The Very Hungry Caterpillar by Eric Carle — Let Hayes hold one side of the book with both hands while you turn pages. Point to each fruit and say "Can you touch the apple?" Encourage him to reach and poke the page.
Motor/Sensory Activity: Give Hayes a medium-sized soft ball that requires two hands to hold. Show him how to squeeze it, lift it, and drop it into a bucket. The two-handed grip strengthens coordination between both sides of his body.
Song/Poem: You Are My Sunshine — Hold a large scarf between you and Hayes, each holding one end. Sway it gently side to side while singing. Let it billow up like a parachute on "sunshine."
Woven Ideas: Let Hayes crinkle and tear large pieces of tissue paper (supervise to prevent mouthing pieces). The two-handed ripping action is novel and the sound is exciting. Say "Rip rip rip! You're so strong!"
Fri — Building anticipation and memory
Read Aloud: I Love You Through and Through by Bernadette Rossetti-Shustak — Read with dramatic pauses. By the third reading this week, leave out the last word of familiar lines and see if Hayes babbles or bounces to fill the gap.
Motor/Sensory Activity: Play peekaboo with a blanket — but this time, put the blanket over HAYES's head (loosely). Say "Where's Hayes?" and wait. He may pull it off himself! The thrill of agency plus the memory that you're still there.
Song/Poem: Rock-a-Bye Baby — Start singing and then suddenly stop. Wait 3–4 seconds. Hayes may vocalize or bounce to request more. When he does, continue singing. This is a beautiful form of early communication.
Woven Ideas: Set up a simple cause-and-effect chain: Hayes pushes a ball off the high chair tray, it lands in a bowl on the floor with a clatter. Retrieve it, hand it back. The predictable sequence builds memory and expectation.
Week 8 — Commando Crawl & Object Permanence
Mon — Commando crawl motivation
Read Aloud: Baby Faces by DK — Place the book a short distance ahead of Hayes on the floor during tummy time. Read the first page, then slide it forward an inch. Narrate: "Come see this baby! You can do it!" Motivate forward movement.
Motor/Sensory Activity: Place Hayes's absolute favorite toy just out of arm's reach on a smooth floor. Get behind him and place your hands flat against his feet so he has something to push off from. Even an inch of forward movement is a triumph.
Song/Poem: Twinkle Twinkle Little Star — Sing from a few feet ahead of Hayes during tummy time. Pat the floor in front of you: "Come to Mama! Twinkle twinkle..." Your voice is the best motivation to move forward.
Woven Ideas: Create a "sensory path" with different fabrics taped to the floor in a line: fleece, burlap, satin, felt. Encourage Hayes to commando-crawl along it. Name each texture as he crosses it.
Tue — Two-handed coordinated play
Read Aloud: Pat the Bunny by Dorothy Kunhardt — Encourage Hayes to use one hand to hold the book open and the other to touch the textures. This coordinated two-handed action is much more advanced than a few weeks ago.
Motor/Sensory Activity: Give Hayes stacking cups. Show him how to nest a small one inside a large one using both hands. Then dump them out. The in-and-out game builds hand coordination and spatial awareness. Let him bang them together too.
Song/Poem: Round and Round the Garden — Do the finger play, but this time try it on Hayes's palm and see if he opens his hand in anticipation. He's learning to participate in the game, not just receive it.
Woven Ideas: Offer Hayes a container with large soft blocks inside. Show him how to take them out one by one. Then show him how to put one back in. The concept of "in" and "out" is a major cognitive milestone.
Wed — Object permanence — beginning

Motor/Sensory Activity: Draw a big square on paper. Guide Hayes's finger along each side, pausing at corners: "Side, corner, side, corner..." Then try a triangle: "One, two, three sides!"
Song/Poem: Humpty Dumpty
Woven Ideas: Use a wet finger to draw shapes on the sidewalk or patio. Watch them disappear and say "Where did the circle go? Let's make another one!"
Fri — Shape hunt — outdoors
Read Aloud: Chicka Chicka Boom Boom by Bill Martin Jr. — Read with bouncy rhythm. After reading, head outside to find shapes in the yard or on a short walk.
Motor/Sensory Activity: On a walk, point out shapes — round wheels on cars, square windows, triangular roof peaks. Let Hayes point when he notices something. Narrate everything.
Song/Poem: Five Little Monkeys
Woven Ideas: Collect round pebbles and square-ish sticks on your walk. Put them in a bag and sort them at home into shape piles on the floor.
Week 4
Mon — Stacking by size — big to small
Read Aloud: Changes Changes by Pat Hutchins — The wooden block people build and rebuild. Point out how shapes stack. Say "big block on the bottom, small on top!"
Motor/Sensory Activity: Gather three containers of different sizes — big, medium, small. Show Hayes how to stack them biggest on bottom. Let him knock them down and try again.
Song/Poem: This Old Man
Woven Ideas: At mealtime, show Hayes a big spoon and a small spoon. Let him hold each and say "big spoon" and "little spoon." Which does he want to use?
Tue — Stacking by size — nesting
Read Aloud: Big Red Barn by Margaret Wise Brown — Reread with a focus on size. "The big barn... the small mouse... the tiny butterfly." Emphasize size words.
Motor/Sensory Activity: Use nesting cups or bowls. Show Hayes how the small one hides inside the big one. Let him try putting them together and pulling them apart.
Song/Poem: One Two Buckle My Shoe
Woven Ideas: Fold laundry together. Hold up a big towel and a small washcloth. Say "big!" and "small!" Let Hayes help push the small one into a pile.
Wed — Shape stamping — circles
Read Aloud: Shapes, Shapes, Shapes by Tana Hoban — Revisit the photographs and count circles together. After reading, transition to the stamping activity.
Motor/Sensory Activity: Cut a sponge into a circle. Pour a thin layer of washable paint on a paper plate. Show Hayes how to dip and stamp circles onto paper. Name the shape each time.
Song/Poem: The Wheels on the Bus
Woven Ideas: While paint dries, wash hands together at the sink. Make circles in the soap suds on his hands. Say "round and round, clean hands!"
Thu — Shape stamping — squares and triangles
Read Aloud: Mouse Shapes by Ellen Stoll Walsh — The mice use shapes to create pictures. Say "Let's make shape pictures too!" after reading.
Motor/Sensory Activity: Cut sponges into a square and a triangle. Let Hayes dip and stamp each one onto paper. Name the shape with each stamp: "Square! Triangle!"
Song/Poem: Jack and Jill
Woven Ideas: Tape the finished shape stamping art on the fridge at Hayes's eye level. Throughout the day, walk by and point: "You made circles and squares and triangles!"
Fri — Size sorting with shape stamps
Read Aloud: Color Zoo by Lois Ehlert — Notice how shapes layer big to small to make animal faces. Say "big circle, then small circle on top!"
Motor/Sensory Activity: Use the sponge stamps from earlier in the week. Make big stamps (press hard and full) and small stamps (just a corner touch). Compare sizes on the paper.
Song/Poem: London Bridge Is Falling Down
Woven Ideas: Gather shoes — a big adult shoe and a small baby shoe. Line them up and say "big shoe, small shoe." Let Hayes try to step into the big shoe for laughs.
Week 5
Mon — Four shapes review — circle, square, triangle, rectangle
Read Aloud: The Shape of Things by Dayle Ann Dodds — Read and pause on each shape. This time, wait a beat and see if Hayes can point to the named shape before you do.
Motor/Sensory Activity: Cut all four shapes from foam or cardboard. Lay them on the floor. Say "Where's the circle?" and cheer when Hayes picks it up or points to it. Go through all four.
Song/Poem: Humpty Dumpty
Woven Ideas: At each meal today, identify one shape on the plate: "Your toast is a rectangle!" Keep it light and fun, just one shape observation per meal.
Tue — In and out with containers
Read Aloud: Not a Box by Antoinette Portis — Read with drama. The box becomes so many things! After reading, explore in and out with a real box.
Motor/Sensory Activity: Set out a large container and small toys. Show Hayes how to put toys IN the container, then dump them OUT. Narrate: "In! Out! In goes the ball! Out comes the block!"
Song/Poem: Five Little Monkeys
Woven Ideas: At bath time, give Hayes cups. Help him scoop water IN the cup, then pour it OUT. Say the words clearly: "Water goes IN... and water comes OUT!"
Wed — On top and under
Read Aloud: Goodnight Moon by Margaret Wise Brown — Read slowly, pointing out where things are: "The kittens are ON the chair. The mouse is ON the floor."
Motor/Sensory Activity: Place a block ON TOP of a box, then UNDER the box. Let Hayes do it. "Put the block ON the box! Now put it UNDER the box!" Celebrate each attempt.
Song/Poem: This Old Man
Woven Ideas: During play, casually narrate positions: "Your truck is ON the rug. Your cup is ON the table. Your shoe is UNDER the chair." Hayes absorbs these spatial words.
Thu — Shapes into containers
Read Aloud: Mouse Shapes by Ellen Stoll Walsh — After reading, set up a shape-sorting challenge using containers.
Motor/Sensory Activity: Label two bowls with a circle sticker and a square sticker. Give Hayes a pile of round lids and square blocks. Help him sort each into the matching bowl.
Song/Poem: One Two Buckle My Shoe
Woven Ideas: Play peekaboo with shapes! Hide a cardboard circle under a cloth. Say "Where did the circle go?" Lift the cloth: "There it is!" Repeat with different shapes.
Fri — In, out, and on — obstacle course
Read Aloud: Chicka Chicka Boom Boom by Bill Martin Jr. — Read with rhythm. The letters climb UP the tree and fall DOWN. Emphasize position words.
Motor/Sensory Activity: Make a mini obstacle course: step IN a hoop (or circle of string), climb ON a cushion, crawl UNDER a blanket draped over chairs. Narrate each position.
Song/Poem: The Wheels on the Bus
Woven Ideas: Let Hayes help put groceries away. Hand him items one at a time: "Put the apple IN the bowl. Put the box ON the shelf." He loves to help!
Week 6
Mon — Shape matching cards
Read Aloud: Circles, Stars, and Squares by Jane Brocket — Look at the real-world shape photos. After reading, set up the matching card activity.
Motor/Sensory Activity: Make simple shape cards — two circles, two squares, two triangles. Lay one set out. Hold up a card and say "Find the match!" Help Hayes place the matching card on top.
Song/Poem: Jack and Jill
Woven Ideas: Stick shape cards on the fridge with magnets. Throughout the day, walk by and ask Hayes to point to shapes: "Where's the triangle?" Praise every attempt.
Tue — Building tall towers
Read Aloud: Changes Changes by Pat Hutchins — Focus on how the block people build tall structures. Say "tall tower!" and use your hand to show height.
Motor/Sensory Activity: Stack blocks as tall as possible together. Count each block as you add it. When it falls, say "Crash! Let's build it tall again!" Aim for 5–6 blocks.
Song/Poem: London Bridge Is Falling Down
Woven Ideas: Stack other things throughout the day — cushions into a pile, books into a tower, plastic cups into a tall column. Say "tall!" and "taller!" each time.
Wed — Shape matching with real objects
Read Aloud: So Many Circles, So Many Squares by Tana Hoban — After reading, gather real objects that match the shapes in the photos.
Motor/Sensory Activity: Lay out a cardboard circle, square, and triangle. Give Hayes real objects — a round lid, a square block, a triangular piece of cheese. Help him place each on its matching shape.
Song/Poem: Humpty Dumpty
Woven Ideas: Play "shape delivery" — ask Hayes to carry a round ball to the kitchen, then a square book to the couch. He practices shapes and following directions.
Thu — Simple puzzles — 2 pieces
Read Aloud: Color Zoo by Lois Ehlert — The layered pages work like a puzzle! Lift flaps and name the shapes that make each animal face.
Motor/Sensory Activity: Cut a simple picture in half (a photo from a magazine or a printed image). Show Hayes how the two pieces fit together. Let him try pushing them into place.
Song/Poem: Five Little Monkeys
Woven Ideas: At snack time, break a cracker in two pieces. Show Hayes how they fit back together: "Two pieces make one whole cracker!" Let him try fitting them.
Fri — Tall towers with shape sorting
Read Aloud: Big Red Barn by Margaret Wise Brown — Read and point out the shapes that make up the barn: "Square door, triangle roof, round sun!"
Motor/Sensory Activity: Build a tower using only square blocks, then only round stacking rings. Compare: "Square blocks stack so well! Round ones are tricky!" Let Hayes experiment.
Song/Poem: This Old Man
Woven Ideas: Before bed, do a quiet shape review. Hold up shape cards one at a time and say the name softly. Kiss each shape goodnight. "Goodnight, circle. Goodnight, square."
Week 7
Mon — Shape collage — torn paper circles
Read Aloud: Round Is a Tortilla by Roseanne Greenfield Thong — Read and enjoy the round things. After reading, make a round collage together.
Motor/Sensory Activity: Tear pieces of colored paper (tearing is great for fine motor!). Help Hayes glue the torn pieces onto a big circle drawn on paper. Say "We're filling the circle!"
Song/Poem: The Wheels on the Bus
Woven Ideas: Save scraps from the torn paper. Let Hayes put them IN a jar and shake it like a maraca. Later, pour them OUT into the recycling together.
Tue — Shape collage — torn paper squares
Read Aloud: Not a Box by Antoinette Portis — Read and then use a real box as a frame for today's square collage.
Motor/Sensory Activity: Draw a big square on paper. Let Hayes tear paper strips and help glue them inside the square. Contrast with yesterday: "Yesterday we filled a circle, today a square!"
Song/Poem: Humpty Dumpty
Woven Ideas: Use the leftover paper scraps to make a trail on the floor. Walk along it together: "Follow the paper path!" Sweep them up together at the end.
Wed — Sorting objects by shape
Read Aloud: Mouse Shapes by Ellen Stoll Walsh — The mice sort shapes to make pictures. Say "Let's sort our shapes too!"
Motor/Sensory Activity: Gather a mix of round lids, square blocks, and triangular items (folded napkin corners). Set out three bowls labeled with shape drawings. Help Hayes sort each item into the correct bowl.
Song/Poem: One Two Buckle My Shoe
Woven Ideas: During snack, offer three types of crackers in different shapes. Ask Hayes to pick out all the round ones first, then the square ones. He sorts and eats!
Thu — Pretend play — cooking with shapes
Read Aloud: Shapes, Shapes, Shapes by Tana Hoban — Point out kitchen shapes in the photos: round pots, square cutting boards, rectangular trays.
Motor/Sensory Activity: Set up a pretend kitchen. Give Hayes play food or real items — round oranges, square cheese slices. Pretend to cook: "Stir the round soup! Flip the square pancake!"
Song/Poem: Jack and Jill
Woven Ideas: Let Hayes help with real cooking. Give him a wooden spoon to stir in a bowl (circle motion). Point out the round bowl and round spoon end.
Fri — Sorting by size and shape combined
Read Aloud: The Shape of Things by Dayle Ann Dodds — Final read this week. See if Hayes can name or point to shapes before you do.
Motor/Sensory Activity: Set out big circles and small circles, big squares and small squares. Ask Hayes to find all the big ones, then all the small ones. Then sort by shape. Two ways to sort!
Song/Poem: Five Little Monkeys
Woven Ideas: Play "shape restaurant" — Hayes serves you shapes on a plate. "I'd like a triangle, please!" Make it silly and fun. Thank him as the chef!
Week 8
Mon — Naming all four shapes — confidence building
Read Aloud: Color Zoo by Lois Ehlert — Go through slowly. Before naming each shape, pause and let Hayes try. Celebrate any attempt — even a babbled sound counts!
Motor/Sensory Activity: Hold up each shape card one at a time. Say the name and have Hayes repeat. Then lay all four on the floor and ask him to pick up each one by name. High fives for each success!
Song/Poem: This Old Man
Woven Ideas: Play "shape spy" during the day. Say "I spy a circle!" and look around together. When Hayes spots one, celebrate big. Take turns being the shape spy.
Tue — Simple two-piece puzzles
Read Aloud: Goodnight Moon by Margaret Wise Brown — A calm, familiar read. Point out shapes in the great green room: "rectangular rug, square window, round moon."
Motor/Sensory Activity: Print or draw simple pictures and cut each into two pieces. Start with just two puzzles. Show Hayes how to push pieces together. Celebrate when the picture appears!
Song/Poem: London Bridge Is Falling Down
Woven Ideas: At lunchtime, cut a piece of toast into two pieces. Say "Two pieces! Can you put them back together?" Let Hayes push them back into a toast puzzle.
Wed — Counting to 3 — introduction
Read Aloud: Chicka Chicka Boom Boom by Bill Martin Jr. — Read with rhythm. After, count three items from the book: "One coconut, two coconuts, three coconuts — boom!"
Motor/Sensory Activity: Line up three blocks. Touch each one and count slowly: "One... two... three!" Let Hayes touch and count with you. Try with other objects — three spoons, three socks.
Song/Poem: One Two Buckle My Shoe
Woven Ideas: Count to three at natural moments: climbing stairs ("one, two, three steps!"), stacking blocks ("one, two, three blocks!"), picking up toys ("one, two, three bears!").
Thu — Shapes and counting combined
Read Aloud: Circles, Stars, and Squares by Jane Brocket — Count the shapes on each page: "One circle, two circles, three circles!" Keep it to three.
Motor/Sensory Activity: Put three circles on the floor. Count them together. Then three squares. Then three triangles. Hayes touches each as you count: "One, two, three triangles!"
Song/Poem: Humpty Dumpty
Woven Ideas: At bath time, float three toys. Count them together. Take one out: "Now there are two!" Take another: "Now there's one!" Add them back and count to three again.
Fri — Unit 6 celebration — shape party!
Read Aloud: Changes Changes by Pat Hutchins — A perfect finale. The blocks transform into so many things. Say "Look at all the shapes Hayes knows now!"
Motor/Sensory Activity: Set up shape stations around the room: a circle station with balls and round lids, a square station with blocks and boxes, a triangle station with folded paper triangles. Let Hayes roam between them freely, playing and naming shapes.
Song/Poem: The Wheels on the Bus
Woven Ideas: Take a photo of Hayes with his favorite shapes. At bedtime, review the week: "You know circles, squares, triangles, and rectangles! You can count to three! You're growing so much!"
