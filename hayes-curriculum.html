Week 1 — Face-to-Face & First Impressions
Mon
Face-to-face connection
📖
Read Aloud
Black & White by Tana Hoban — Hold the book 20–30cm from Hayes. Slowly turn pages and pause on each bold pattern, letting him study the contrast.
💪
Motor Activity
Tummy time on a firm surface for 2–3 minutes. Get down at Hayes's level and smile at him so he lifts his head to see your face.
🎵
Song / Poem
Twinkle Twinkle Little Star — Sing slowly while making eye contact. Gently tap his hands together on the beat.
🌿
Woven Ideas
While changing Hayes's diaper, narrate what you see: "I see your toes! One, two, three..." This builds language even in routine moments.
Tue
High contrast exploration
📖
Read Aloud
Baby Faces by DK — Point to each baby's face and name the emotion: "This baby is happy! Look at that smile." Pause and let Hayes study each photo.
💪
Motor Activity
Place a high-contrast card (black and white stripes or bullseye) propped up during tummy time. Let Hayes lift his head and look at it for 2–3 minutes.
🎵
Song / Poem
You Are My Sunshine — Sing while gently stroking Hayes's cheeks and arms. Slow the tempo to keep it soothing.
🌿
Woven Ideas
At bathtime, gently splash the water and say "splash!" each time. Repetition of cause and effect starts here.
Wed
Gentle sensory awakening
📖
Read Aloud
Pat the Bunny by Dorothy Kunhardt — Guide Hayes's hand to touch the bunny's fur and the scratchy face. Say "soft!" and "scratchy!" with animated expressions.
💪
Motor Activity
Lay Hayes on his back and gently bicycle his legs, singing a slow rhythm. Do 5–6 gentle cycles, then pause and smile.
🎵
Song / Poem
Round and Round the Garden — Trace circles on Hayes's palm, then "walk" your fingers up his arm. Pause before the tickle so he learns to anticipate.
🌿
Woven Ideas
Hold Hayes facing outward at a window. Describe what you see outside: "I see a tree! The leaves are green." Fresh scenery feeds curiosity.
Thu
Babbling back-and-forth
📖
Read Aloud
Global Babies by The Global Fund for Children — Show each page and say "Hello, baby!" in a warm voice. Pause after each page and wait to see if Hayes coos or babbles back.
💪
Motor Activity
Tummy time with a rolled-up towel under Hayes's chest for extra support. Place your face at his level and make silly sounds to encourage him to lift and hold his head.
🎵
Song / Poem
Rock-a-Bye Baby — Rock Hayes gently in your arms while singing. Sway side to side in a slow, predictable rhythm.
🌿
Woven Ideas
During a feed, pause and say "Mmm, yummy!" then wait. If Hayes makes a sound, respond enthusiastically: "You think so too!" This models turn-taking.
Fri
Playful connection & review
📖
Read Aloud
Peek-a-Boo! by Roberta Grobel Intrater — Cover your face with the book, then reveal with a big "Peek-a-boo!" Watch for Hayes's reaction and repeat if he smiles.
💪
Motor Activity
Hold Hayes in a supported standing position on your lap for 30 seconds, letting him bear a little weight on his legs. Bounce gently and cheer him on.
🎵
Song / Poem
If You're Happy and You Know It — Clap Hayes's hands together gently on "clap your hands." Use an excited voice for each verse.
🌿
Woven Ideas
Blow gentle raspberries on Hayes's belly during a diaper change. Pause and see if he tries to vocalize or laugh in response. Celebrate any sounds he makes.
Week 2 — Mirror Play & Babbling
Mon
Mirror discovery
📖
Read Aloud
Baby Faces by DK — Hold the book next to a small mirror. Point to a smiling baby in the book, then point to Hayes's reflection: "That's you! You're smiling too!"
💪
Motor Activity
Tummy time in front of a baby-safe mirror propped on the floor. Tap the mirror and say Hayes's name so he lifts his head to look. Aim for 3 minutes.
🎵
Song / Poem
Twinkle Twinkle Little Star — Sing in front of the mirror so Hayes can see both your face and his own. Point to his reflection on the word "star."
🌿
Woven Ideas
Carry Hayes around the house and stop at different mirrors. Say "Who's that? It's Hayes!" each time. Familiarity with his own reflection supports self-awareness.
Tue
Extended tummy time
📖
Read Aloud
Hello, World! by Jill McDonald — Read with bright enthusiasm, pointing out colors on each page: "Look, the sun is yellow!" Let Hayes touch the pages if he reaches.
💪
Motor Activity
Tummy time for 4–5 minutes. Place a crinkle toy just within reach in front of Hayes. Encourage him by patting the toy so it makes noise.
🎵
Song / Poem
Row Row Row Your Boat — Hold Hayes's hands and gently rock him forward and back in a seated position on your lap as you sing.
🌿
Woven Ideas
Crinkle a piece of tissue paper near Hayes's ear (not too close). Watch his reaction, then let him grab it with supervision. New textures and sounds spark neural connections.
Wed
Babbling dialogue
📖
Read Aloud
Goodnight Moon by Margaret Wise Brown — Read in a hushed, rhythmic voice. Pause at the end of each "Goodnight..." line and wait for Hayes to babble or coo before continuing.
💪
Motor Activity
Place Hayes on his back. Hold a soft rattle above his chest and shake it gently. When he reaches up, let him grab it. Repeat 3–4 times to encourage arm reaching.
🎵
Song / Poem
Baa Baa Black Sheep — Sing with exaggerated mouth movements so Hayes can see how your lips form each sound. Pause after "Yes sir, yes sir" to invite a babble.
🌿
Woven Ideas
During a quiet moment, imitate any sound Hayes makes. If he says "ahh," you say "ahh" right back. Wait. If he does it again, copy again. This is the foundation of conversation.
Thu
Sensory textures
📖
Read Aloud
Baby Touch and Feel: Animals by DK — Guide Hayes's fingers over each textured patch. Name the animal and the feeling: "The kitten is soft. The lizard is bumpy!"
💪
Motor Activity
Tummy time with a play mat. Place two different textured cloths (smooth satin, soft fleece) in front of Hayes so he can reach and feel them while propping up on his forearms.
🎵
Song / Poem
The Itsy Bitsy Spider — Walk your fingers up Hayes's arm for the spider climbing. Wiggle your fingers down for the rain. Use big facial expressions throughout.
🌿
Woven Ideas
Let Hayes hold a cool metal spoon and a warm soft cloth (warmed briefly in your hands). Say "cool!" and "warm!" as he explores each one. Temperature variety builds sensory vocabulary.
Fri
Social smiling
📖
Read Aloud
I Love You Through and Through by Bernadette Rossetti-Shustak — Read with warm affection. Touch Hayes's toes when the book mentions toes, his hair when it mentions hair. Make it physical and connected.
💪
Motor Activity
Sit Hayes on your lap facing you, supporting him at the hips. Make funny faces — stick out your tongue, puff your cheeks, open your mouth wide. See if he tries to copy any expression.
🎵
Song / Poem
You Are My Sunshine — Sing verse one while holding Hayes close, then hold him at arm's length and beam a big smile at him. Alternate close and far throughout the song.
🌿
Woven Ideas
Play a gentle game where you hide behind a burp cloth and pop out with "Boo!" Keep the cloth small so Hayes can still see your outline. Early disappearance-and-return games build trust.
Week 3 — Reaching & Rolling
Mon
Reaching for a dangling toy
📖
Read Aloud
The Very Hungry Caterpillar by Eric Carle — Slowly move your finger through each hole in the pages. Name each food with relish: "One apple — crunch!" Encourage Hayes to touch the pages.
💪
Motor Activity
Hang a lightweight toy (rattle or fabric ring) from your hand about 15cm above Hayes while he lies on his back. Wiggle it gently and encourage him to swat and grab at it.
🎵
Song / Poem
Twinkle Twinkle Little Star — Hold a small star-shaped toy or cut-out while singing. Move it slowly in a gentle arc and let Hayes reach for it during the song.
🌿
Woven Ideas
Place a toy slightly to Hayes's side during tummy time — not directly ahead but at a 45-degree angle. This nudges him to shift his weight, a precursor to rolling.
Tue
Rolling practice
📖
Read Aloud
Where Is Baby's Belly Button? by Karen Katz — Lift each flap with suspense: "Where are baby's eyes?" Pause, then reveal. Let Hayes help lift flaps if he reaches for them.
💪
Motor Activity
Place Hayes on his back and hold a toy to one side, near his hip. Gently guide him to roll from back to side by bending his top knee across. Cheer when he gets to his side. Try both directions.
🎵
Song / Poem
Round and Round the Garden — After the finger play on his palm, try it on his tummy. The anticipation of the tickle will make him squirm and engage his core.
🌿
Woven Ideas
When Hayes drops a toy, say "Uh-oh! It fell down!" and pick it up. Repeat. Naming what happens builds language around cause and effect, and he may start dropping things on purpose soon.
Wed
Voice localization
📖
Read Aloud
Global Babies by The Global Fund for Children — Read from slightly to one side of Hayes, then shift to the other side. Notice if he turns toward your voice. Say "I'm over here!" when you move.
💪
Motor Activity
During tummy time, call Hayes's name from different positions — left side, right side, slightly behind. Encourage him to push up and turn his head toward your voice. Keep sessions to 3–4 minutes.
🎵
Song / Poem
Rock-a-Bye Baby — Sing from across the room, then move closer. Vary your distance so Hayes practices locating where the sound comes from.
🌿
Woven Ideas
Shake a small bell or rattle behind a cloth. When Hayes looks curious, reveal it and say "You found it!" Associating sound with source is a key cognitive step.
Thu
Grasping practice
📖
Read Aloud
Pat the Bunny by Dorothy Kunhardt — This time, let Hayes take the lead. Place the book in front of him and let him pat, grab, and mouth it. Narrate what he's doing: "You're touching the bunny!"
💪
Motor Activity
Offer Hayes a soft fabric ring. When he grabs it, gently tug — just enough so he feels resistance and grips tighter. Release and celebrate: "Strong grip!" Repeat with each hand.
🎵
Song / Poem
If You're Happy and You Know It — Place a rattle in Hayes's hand and shake his hand gently on "clap your hands." Switch to stomping his feet on the next verse.
🌿
Woven Ideas
Place a few safe kitchen items on a towel in front of Hayes — a wooden spoon, a silicone spatula, a small whisk. Let him grab and mouth each one. Everyday objects are the best toys at this age.
Fri
Supported mini-sits
📖
Read Aloud
Ten Little Fingers and Ten Little Toes by Mem Fox — Touch Hayes's fingers and toes as you read. Count along slowly. The rhyming pattern is soothing and predictable — pause before the rhyming word to build anticipation.
💪
Motor Activity
Sit Hayes in a Boppy pillow or between your legs on the floor. Place a toy in front of him and let him lean forward to reach it. Support him as needed, letting him practice balancing for 1–2 minutes.
🎵
Song / Poem
Baa Baa Black Sheep — Bounce Hayes gently on your knee to the beat. Emphasize the "baa baa" by nodding your head dramatically so he can see the rhythm.
🌿
Woven Ideas
Hold Hayes in front of a running faucet (test temperature first). Let the water trickle over his fingers. Say "water! Splashy splash!" New sensory experiences in safe contexts build confidence.
Week 4 — Sitting & Cause-and-Effect
Mon
Supported sitting exploration
📖
Read Aloud
Black & White by Tana Hoban — Prop Hayes in a supported sit and hold the book in front of him. Now that he's more upright, the perspective changes. Let him reach out and bat the pages.
💪
Motor Activity
Sit Hayes between your legs on the floor with toys scattered in a semicircle around him. Support his trunk lightly and let him lean to reach different toys. This builds core strength and balance.
🎵
Song / Poem
Row Row Row Your Boat — Sit facing Hayes, hold his hands, and gently pull him forward and lean him back with each "row." This is a mini sit-up workout disguised as a song!
🌿
Woven Ideas
Fill a small container with dried pasta (large shells, supervised). Let Hayes reach in and grab handfuls. The sound, texture, and weight are all novel. Narrate: "Scoopy scoop! You got some!"
Tue
Cause-and-effect discovery
📖
Read Aloud
Peek-a-Boo! by Roberta Grobel Intrater — Let Hayes hold one side of the book while you hold the other. When he accidentally closes it, say "Where did the baby go?" and reopen it together.
💪
Motor Activity
Place a toy on top of a small cloth in front of Hayes. Show him that pulling the cloth brings the toy closer. Guide his hand the first time, then see if he tries independently.
🎵
Song / Poem
The Itsy Bitsy Spider — Use a popup toy or jack-in-the-box alongside the song. Pop it up at "out came the sun." The surprise reinforces cause and effect.
🌿
Woven Ideas
Tape a sheet of bubble wrap to the high chair tray (edges folded under for safety). Let Hayes bang and press it. Every pop is instant feedback — cause and effect in action.
Wed
Turn-taking vocalizations
📖
Read Aloud
Goodnight Moon by Margaret Wise Brown — Whisper the goodnight lines and pause. If Hayes makes any sound, respond with "Goodnight, Hayes." Treat every babble as a real word in the conversation.
💪
Motor Activity
Place a ball in front of Hayes during supported sitting. Roll it gently toward him and say "Your turn!" If he bats it, say "You rolled it! My turn!" Roll it back. Early turn-taking through play.
🎵
Song / Poem
You Are My Sunshine — Sing the first line, then stop and wait. If Hayes coos or babbles, say "Beautiful singing!" and continue. Stop-and-wait builds anticipation and encourages vocalization.
🌿
Woven Ideas
During meal prep, hold Hayes on your hip and narrate: "I'm cutting a banana — chop chop chop! It's yellow and soft." Everyday narration is the single most powerful language builder.
Thu
Pressing and pushing
📖
Read Aloud
Baby Touch and Feel: Animals by DK — This time focus on the sounds: "The cow says moo! The duck says quack!" Use big pauses between animals and watch if Hayes babbles his own "animal sounds" in response.
💪
Motor Activity
Offer Hayes a toy with a big button that lights up or makes noise when pressed. Guide his hand to push it the first time. Watch his face when it activates — then let him figure it out alone.
🎵
Song / Poem
Twinkle Twinkle Little Star — Use a light-up toy that activates with a press. Help Hayes press it at key moments in the song: "like a diamond in the sky" — press! Light!
🌿
Woven Ideas
Blow up a balloon (keep it in your hands at all times for safety). Let Hayes tap it. It moves in unpredictable ways — fascinating for a baby learning that their actions cause reactions.
Fri
Interactive play consolidation
📖
Read Aloud
Hello, World! by Jill McDonald — Read with Hayes sitting on your lap facing the book. Let him turn pages (with help). Celebrate each turn: "You turned the page! What's next?"
💪
Motor Activity
Create a mini obstacle: a rolled towel for Hayes to lean over from a sitting position to grab a toy on the other side. Support him as he stretches, building core and confidence.
🎵
Song / Poem
Round and Round the Garden — Do the finger play on Hayes's belly, then try it on the sole of his foot. Different body parts keep the same game feeling brand new.
🌿
Woven Ideas
Play "So Big!" — ask "How big is Hayes?" and stretch his arms up: "Sooooo big!" Repeat several times. He may start raising his arms on his own by the end of the week.
Week 5 — Reaching While Sitting & Imitation
Mon
Reaching while sitting
📖
Read Aloud
Where Is Baby's Belly Button? by Karen Katz — Sit Hayes up and hold the book slightly to one side so he has to twist his trunk to see the flaps. Alternate sides with each page.
💪
Motor Activity
Sit Hayes with support and hold toys at different heights — one at chest level, one higher near his head, one to the side. Encourage him to reach in all directions while maintaining his balance.
🎵
Song / Poem
If You're Happy and You Know It — During "clap your hands," hold your hands up high so Hayes has to reach up to clap with you. This challenges his sitting balance while he moves his arms.
🌿
Woven Ideas
Fill a small zip-lock bag with hair gel and a few drops of food coloring (seal it tight with tape). Let Hayes squish and press it on his high chair tray. Mess-free sensory play!
Tue
Banging objects together
📖
Read Aloud
The Very Hungry Caterpillar by Eric Carle — Give Hayes a small wooden block to hold while you read. When the caterpillar eats, encourage Hayes to bang the block on the table: "Munch munch munch!"
💪
Motor Activity
Give Hayes two small wooden blocks (or stacking cups). Show him how to bang them together — clap them and make a big "BANG!" sound. Hand them over and cheer any attempt to imitate.
🎵
Song / Poem
Baa Baa Black Sheep — Give Hayes a wooden spoon and a pot lid. Bang the "drum" on the beat of the song. Let him take over the drumming while you sing.
🌿
Woven Ideas
Let Hayes bang a spoon on different surfaces: the high chair tray, a book, a cushion. Name each sound: "That's a loud bang! That's a soft thud!" He's learning physics through play.
Wed
Imitating mouth movements
📖
Read Aloud
Baby Faces by DK — Focus on the mouths in each photo. Open your mouth wide and say "ahhh," then purse your lips and say "ooo." Watch if Hayes tries to copy your mouth shapes.
💪
Motor Activity
Sit face-to-face with Hayes. Stick out your tongue slowly — hold it — then pull it back in. Repeat 3–4 times. Try big open mouth, puffed cheeks, and lip smacking. Celebrate any attempt to imitate.
🎵
Song / Poem
Row Row Row Your Boat — Exaggerate your mouth on each word. "Roooow" with a big round mouth, "merrily" with a wide smile. Sing slowly enough that Hayes can study your face.
🌿
Woven Ideas
Blow raspberries on Hayes's arm, then on your own arm so he can see and hear. Say "Your turn!" and wait. Many babies start imitating raspberries this week — it builds oral motor skills for speech.
Thu
Bilateral hand play
📖
Read Aloud
I Love You Through and Through by Bernadette Rossetti-Shustak — Clap Hayes's hands together at the end of each line. By mid-book, pause and see if he claps on his own to continue the pattern.
💪
Motor Activity
Give Hayes a small soft ball. Show him how to pass it from one hand to the other. Gently guide the transfer if needed. Say "This hand... now THIS hand!" Left-right-left.
🎵
Song / Poem
Twinkle Twinkle Little Star — Hold a scarf in front of Hayes with both your hands. Slowly lift it up on "up above the world so high." Drop it on his lap. Let him grab it with both hands and lift it himself.
🌿
Woven Ideas
Offer Hayes a piece of banana or soft pear to hold (if eating solids). Holding food and bringing it to his own mouth is fantastic bilateral coordination and builds independence at mealtimes.
Fri
Vocal imitation games
📖
Read Aloud
Ten Little Fingers and Ten Little Toes by Mem Fox — Read with animated pitch changes. Go high on "fingers" and low on "toes." Wiggle Hayes's fingers and toes in rhythm with the words.
💪
Motor Activity
Place Hayes in supported sitting with a scarf draped over a toy in front of him. Say "Where did it go?" and encourage him to pull the scarf off with both hands to reveal the toy. Repeat with different toys.
🎵
Song / Poem
The Itsy Bitsy Spider — Slow the song way down. Emphasize each syllable. After "itsy bitsy," pause and wait. See if Hayes makes a sound to urge you to continue. Any sound counts!
🌿
Woven Ideas
Record Hayes's babbling on your phone and play it back to him. Watch his face — he may recognize his own voice! This delightful moment builds self-awareness and interest in sounds.
Week 6 — Pushing Up & Mouthing
Mon
Pushing up on hands
📖
Read Aloud
Pat the Bunny by Dorothy Kunhardt — Place the book flat on the floor during tummy time. Hayes can look down at it while pushing up on his hands. Turn pages for him and keep the interaction going.
💪
Motor Activity
During tummy time, place your hands flat on the floor in front of Hayes so he can see them. Push up and say "Up!" Encourage him to straighten his arms and lift his chest fully off the floor. Aim for 4–5 good push-ups.
🎵
Song / Poem
You Are My Sunshine — Sing during tummy time to keep Hayes motivated. Get low on the floor so he can see your face. Your presence is the best incentive to push up and hold.
🌿
Woven Ideas
Place a few drops of vanilla extract on a cotton ball (don't let Hayes touch it directly). Waft it near his nose and say "Mmm, vanilla! Smells sweet!" Introducing varied smells builds sensory pathways.
Tue
Grasping and mouthing
📖
Read Aloud
Baby Touch and Feel: Animals by DK — Let Hayes hold the book himself. He will likely mouth the corners — that's fine! Board books are designed for this. Narrate: "You're tasting the book! Silly Hayes!"
💪
Motor Activity
Offer Hayes 3–4 safe items of different textures: a rubber teether, a wooden ring, a crinkle cloth, a silicone spoon. Let him pick up each one, mouth it, drop it, and choose another. No rush — this is exploration.
🎵
Song / Poem
Rock-a-Bye Baby — Hold Hayes in your arms and gently sway while he holds a teething ring. The comfort of the song plus the oral input from the teether is a perfect soothing combo.
🌿
Woven Ideas
Freeze a damp washcloth and let Hayes gnaw on it (supervise closely). Say "Cold! Brrr!" The temperature and texture provide intense sensory input that most babies love, especially if teething.
Wed
Core strength on tummy
📖
Read Aloud
Global Babies by The Global Fund for Children — Hold the book upright on the floor during tummy time, a few inches in front of Hayes. He'll need to push up high to see the photos. Turn pages slowly.
💪
Motor Activity
Place a toy just slightly out of reach during tummy time. Encourage Hayes to stretch forward on his hands to grab it. If he scoots or army-crawls even a tiny bit, celebrate enormously.
🎵
Song / Poem
Round and Round the Garden — Do the finger play on Hayes's back while he's on his tummy. The tickle at the end will make him squirm and push up — sneaky core workout!
🌿
Woven Ideas
Let Hayes play with a wooden spoon dipped in a tiny bit of yogurt (if eating solids). He can mouth it, wave it, bang it. Multi-sensory play with a simple prop.
Thu
Social laughter
📖
Read Aloud
Peek-a-Boo! by Roberta Grobel Intrater — Build anticipation before each reveal by saying "Wheeeere's the baby..." in a rising voice. Pause longer each time. Hayes may start giggling before you even turn the page.
💪
Motor Activity
Play airplane: lie on your back with knees bent, place Hayes on your shins (hold him securely at the sides), and gently bounce and sway. Make airplane sounds. This is a laugher guaranteed.
🎵
Song / Poem
If You're Happy and You Know It — Add a silly verse: "If you're happy and you know it, boop your nose!" and gently boop Hayes's nose. Surprise variations get the biggest laughs.
🌿
Woven Ideas
Play the "gonna get you" game: slowly walk your fingers toward Hayes, saying "I'm gonna get youuuu..." then tickle gently. The slow approach builds delicious anticipation and social laughter.
Fri
Reaching from push-up position
📖
Read Aloud
Black & White by Tana Hoban — Hold the book upright and slowly move it in a gentle arc left and right while Hayes is on his tummy in a push-up position. He'll shift weight to reach toward it.
💪
Motor Activity
During tummy time push-up, place a toy to Hayes's right. When he reaches for it with his right hand (bearing weight on the left), move it to the left side. This encourages weight shifting and builds shoulder stability.
🎵
Song / Poem
Baa Baa Black Sheep — Sing while Hayes is on his tummy. Tap the floor in front of him to the beat, encouraging him to lift one hand and tap along. Even an attempt counts!
🌿
Woven Ideas
Take Hayes outside on a blanket for tummy time. The grass, breeze, and outdoor sounds are a completely different sensory environment. Narrate everything: "Feel the wind? The grass is tickly!"
Week 7 — Pivoting & Transferring
Mon
Pivoting on tummy
📖
Read Aloud
Goodnight Moon by Margaret Wise Brown — Place the book to one side during tummy time. Read a page, then move the book to the other side for the next page. Hayes will pivot to follow the story.
💪
Motor Activity
Place toys in a circle around Hayes while he's on his tummy. Encourage him to pivot (spin on his belly) to reach each toy in turn. Cheer each rotation: "You turned! There's the rattle!"
🎵
Song / Poem
Twinkle Twinkle Little Star — Sit at different positions around Hayes during tummy time and sing a line from each spot. He'll pivot to find you — a fun game of musical hide-and-seek.
🌿
Woven Ideas
Place a few ice cubes on Hayes's high chair tray. He'll try to grab them and they'll slide away — hilarious and great for fine motor persistence. Narrate: "Slippery! Try again!"
Tue
Transferring objects hand to hand

Unit 4: Colours
11–13 months · 8 weeks
Colours & early sorting. Hayes may be cruising or taking first steps. This unit introduces colour names through play, art, and sorting — keeping it fun and pressure-free.

Counting to 3 — introduction
📖
Read Aloud
Chicka Chicka Boom Boom by Bill Martin Jr. — Read with rhythm. After, count three items from the book: "One coconut, two coconuts, three coconuts — boom!"
💪
Motor Activity
Line up three blocks. Touch each one and count slowly: "One... two... three!" Let Hayes touch and count with you. Try with other objects — three spoons, three socks.
🎵
Song / Poem
One Two Buckle My Shoe
🌿
Woven Ideas
Count to three at natural moments: climbing stairs ("one, two, three steps!"), stacking blocks ("one, two, three blocks!"), picking up toys ("one, two, three bears!").
Thu
Shapes and counting combined
📖
Read Aloud
Circles, Stars, and Squares by Jane Brocket — Count the shapes on each page: "One circle, two circles, three circles!" Keep it to three.
💪
Motor Activity
Put three circles on the floor. Count them together. Then three squares. Then three triangles. Hayes touches each as you count: "One, two, three triangles!"

Song / Poem
Humpty Dumpty
🌿
Woven Ideas
At bath time, float three toys. Count them together. Take one out: "Now there are two!" Take another: "Now there's one!" Add them back and count to three again.
