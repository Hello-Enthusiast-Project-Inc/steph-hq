Module 1 — Record-Keeping
Completed June 13, 2026 · Professional Standard

Principle 1 — Creating & Maintaining Accurate Records

›
Q1a: Does each patient require a separate health care record?
Yes.
Q1b: What other records must be maintained separately?
Daily appointment log and billing records/receipts.
In Your Practice
Yes, a patient could have one shared record in a multi-disciplinary setting. Paper must be in a locked file. Electronic must be on a password-protected computer and stored securely in the cloud.
Q2: What information is missing from a record that only has name and treatment?
Sex/gender, DOB, contact info, emergency contact, medical history, consent, assessment, communications (reports, emails, texts, correspondence pertinent to care).
In Your Practice
Has a procedure but could be better for texts and emails related to scheduling changes — needs to be added more consistently.
Q3: What is required when completing health care records?
Complete records at time of visit or as soon as practical afterward, ensuring they are dated, signed, and legible.
In Your Practice
Very strict about this. Leaves time between each patient to ensure records are completed before the next patient arrives.
Q4: How do you correct an error in a health care record?
Make the change and initial it, ensuring the original entry is not removed. For digital records: create a new entry stating the change, referencing the original date, then sign and date the new entry.
In Your Practice
Since notes are online, creates a new dated and signed entry referencing and correcting the original. Preserves integrity of the record.
Q5: Policy for records documented in a language other than English?
Documents in English only — standard is already met. Has a legal contact available to produce certified translated copies if ever needed.

Principle 2 — Storing, Retaining & Disposing Securely

›
Q1: How many years must a patient's health care record be stored?
16 years from date of last entry, or until the patient reaches the age of majority — whichever is later.
In Your Practice — Three-Tier System
Current records in Jane App (digital, secure) · Pre-digital records in locked file cabinet · Future archiving: downloaded to hard drive, secured in locked cabinet.
Q2: What disposal requirements must be met?
Permanently and securely destroyed so information cannot be retrieved or reconstructed. Paper: shred. Digital: secure data destruction software required.
In Your Practice
Paper: shredded. Digital: still need to source secure data destruction software — this is an identified gap. ⚠️
Q3: Patient privacy and confidentiality is governed by which legislative act?
Personal Information Protection Act (PIPA).
In Your Practice
Obtains a signed consent form before releasing records to any third party.
Q4: Another licensee requests a former patient's health care records — what do you do?
Contact the patient and have them complete a consent form before releasing anything. Documentation required: the signed consent form.
Q5: Switching digital health care record providers — what is required?
New provider must comply with PIPA, have appropriate physical/electronic/administrative safeguards, securely transfer records, limit access to authorized individuals, and maintain documentation of storage and security measures.
In Your Practice
Uses Jane App — a Canadian, PIPA-compliant platform with encrypted storage and strong access controls. ✅

Principle 3 — Responding to Record Requests

›
Q1: How long do you have to provide a copy of a health care record?
30 business days.
In Your Practice
Acknowledges the request, explains the record fee, and provides records as soon as possible.
Q2: How much can you charge for producing a copy of a health care record?
A reasonable amount.
In Your Practice
$25 flat fee for all patients. Reflects 30 min–1 hr retrieval time. Consistent, equal for all, and significantly less than the cost of a treatment.

Principle 4 — Handling Personal Information Lawfully

›
Q1: What are the two acceptable reasons for collecting personal information?
1. To provide health care (only what is reasonably required)
2. To fulfill legal obligations
In Your Practice
Reviews intake form annually. Will do a thorough review before returning from maternity leave.
Q2: What three things must you tell a patient about collection of their personal information?
1. What information is being collected
2. Why it is being collected
3. How it will be used
In Your Practice
Patients can see the form when filling it out. Explains why and how verbally. Action item: add a brief written statement to the top of the Jane App intake form covering all three. ⚠️
Q3: Can you disclose treatment info to a practitioner not involved in the patient's care?
No — patient consent is required first.
In Your Practice
Almost never asked. Obtains consent every time it is requested. ✅
Q4: Is emailing patients about an unrelated charity fundraiser acceptable?
No — patient contact info was collected for health care purposes only. Using it for unrelated purposes violates PIPA.
In Your Practice
Only emails patients about matters directly related to their treatment. ✅
Q5: A colleague leaves their tablet open with records visible in a shared lunchroom. Which requirement is not being met?
Security and confidentiality of personal health information — unauthorized individuals in a shared space could view patient records, violating PIPA.
In Your Practice
Auto-lock enabled on all devices with a very short timeout. ✅

Advertising & Marketing — Principle 1: Professional, Accurate, Clear & Verifiable

›
Q1a: What type of advertising tactic is: "Don't wait until your pain becomes unbearable. Book now before it's too late!"
✅ Standard Answer
Pressure-based tactic. Also exploits the emotional and psychological vulnerability of intended recipients. Prohibited under Principle 1.9.
In Your Practice
Fear-inducing / threatening — makes it feel like patients have no choice but to book. No pressure tactics used in your advertising.
Q1b: Why should you not use this type of advertising?
✅ Standard Answer
It takes advantage of the physical, emotional, or psychological vulnerability of intended recipients and uses pressure-based tactics. (Principle 1.9.1 and 1.9.2)
In Your Practice
Manipulates patients into booking out of fear rather than genuine need — undermines trust and clinical integrity.
Q2: Which option best aligns with the professional standard when designing advertising content?
✅ Standard Answer
B — Verifiable and accurate. (Principle 1.2: Ensure the information included in advertising and marketing is accurate, clear, and verifiable.)
In Your Practice
Keep it minimal and straightforward. Include full name and credentials so patients can look you up in the registry and verify who you are.
Q3a: What are you required to keep when it comes to advertising materials?
✅ Standard Answer (Principle 1.11)
— A copy of the publication
— A recording of any broadcast through electronic media (email, social media, website, radio, TV)
— A written record of when and where it was published or broadcast
— Evidence verifying any statements or claims made
In Your Practice
Advertising has been minimal — going forward: save screenshots of anything posted and log the date and platform.
Q3b: How long must you keep advertising and marketing materials?
✅ Standard Answer
3 years from the date of publication or broadcast. (Principle 1.11)
