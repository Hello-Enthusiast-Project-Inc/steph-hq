Principle 1 — Creating & Maintaining Accurate Records

›
Q1a: Does each patient require a separate health care record?
Yes.
Q1b: What other records must be maintained separately?
Daily appointment log and billing records/receipts.
In Your Practice
Yes, a patient could have one shared record in a multi-disciplinary setting. Paper must be in a locked file. Electronic must be on a password-protected computer and stored securely in the cloud.
Q2: What information is missing from a record that only has name and treatment?
Sex/gender, DOB, contact info, emergency contact, medical history, consent, assessment, communications (reports, emails, texts, correspondence pertinent to care).
In Your Practice
Has a procedure but could be better for texts and emails related to scheduling changes — needs to be added more consistently.
Q3: What is required when completing health care records?
Complete records at time of visit or as soon as practical afterward, ensuring they are dated, signed, and legible.
In Your Practice
Very strict about this. Leaves time between each patient to ensure records are completed before the next patient arrives.
Q4: How do you correct an error in a health care record?
Make the change and initial it, ensuring the original entry is not removed. For digital records: create a new entry stating the change, referencing the original date, then sign and date the new entry.
In Your Practice
Since notes are online, creates a new dated and signed entry referencing and correcting the original. Preserves integrity of the record.
Q5: Policy for records documented in a language other than English?
Documents in English only — standard is already met. Has a legal contact available to produce certified translated copies if ever needed.

Principle 2 — Storing, Retaining & Disposing Securely

›
Q1: How many years must a patient's health care record be stored?
16 years from date of last entry, or until the patient reaches the age of majority — whichever is later.
In Your Practice — Three-Tier System
Current records in Jane App (digital, secure) · Pre-digital records in locked file cabinet · Future archiving: downloaded to hard drive, secured in locked cabinet.
Q2: What disposal requirements must be met?
Permanently and securely destroyed so information cannot be retrieved or reconstructed. Paper: shred. Digital: secure data destruction software required.
In Your Practice
Paper: shredded. Digital: still need to source secure data destruction software — this is an identified gap. ⚠️
Q3: Patient privacy and confidentiality is governed by which legislative act?
Personal Information Protection Act (PIPA).
In Your Practice
Obtains a signed consent form before releasing records to any third party.
Q4: Another licensee requests a former patient's health care records — what do you do?
Contact the patient and have them complete a consent form before releasing anything. Documentation required: the signed consent form.
Q5: Switching digital health care record providers — what is required?
New provider must comply with PIPA, have appropriate physical/electronic/administrative safeguards, securely transfer records, limit access to authorized individuals, and maintain documentation of storage and security measures.
In Your Practice
Uses Jane App — a Canadian, PIPA-compliant platform with encrypted storage and strong access controls. ✅

Advertising & Marketing — Principle 1: Professional, Accurate, Clear & Verifiable

›
Q1a: What type of advertising tactic is: "Don't wait until your pain becomes unbearable. Book now before it's too late!"
✅ Standard Answer
Pressure-based tactic. Also exploits the emotional and psychological vulnerability of intended recipients. Prohibited under Principle 1.9.
In Your Practice
Fear-inducing / threatening — makes it feel like patients have no choice but to book. No pressure tactics used in your advertising.
Q1b: Why should you not use this type of advertising?
✅ Standard Answer
It takes advantage of the physical, emotional, or psychological vulnerability of intended recipients and uses pressure-based tactics. (Principle 1.9.1 and 1.9.2)
In Your Practice
Manipulates patients into booking out of fear rather than genuine need — undermines trust and clinical integrity.
Q2: Which option best aligns with the professional standard when designing advertising content?
✅ Standard Answer
B — Verifiable and accurate. (Principle 1.2: Ensure the information included in advertising and marketing is accurate, clear, and verifiable.)
In Your Practice
Keep it minimal and straightforward. Include full name and credentials so patients can look you up in the registry and verify who you are.
Q3a: What are you required to keep when it comes to advertising materials?
✅ Standard Answer (Principle 1.11)
— A copy of the publication
— A recording of any broadcast through electronic media (email, social media, website, radio, TV)
— A written record of when and where it was published or broadcast
— Evidence verifying any statements or claims made
In Your Practice
Advertising has been minimal — going forward: save screenshots of anything posted and log the date and platform.
Q3b: How long must you keep advertising and marketing materials?
✅ Standard Answer
3 years from the date of publication or broadcast. (Principle 1.11)
Q4: When are you NOT permitted to use your reserved title in advertising?
✅ Standard Answer
You are not permitted to use your reserved title to promote services or activities that fall outside your scope of practice. (Principle 1.6)
In Your Practice
RMT title is only used for massage therapy advertising. Pilates business is registered separately and marketed completely independently — "RMT" and CCHPBC registration number are never used in Pilates advertising.
Q5: Which statements should you NOT use? Select all that apply.
✅ Correct: A, C, D
A — "Specializing in chronic pain" → Cannot use "specialist" or similar designations (Principle 1.7)
C — "Better than physiotherapy" → Cannot use comparative language implying superiority (Principle 1.4)
D — "100% success rate" → Unsubstantiated claim creating unjustified expectation (Principle 1.3.1)
B is acceptable — honest, accurate, doesn't overclaim ✅
In Your Practice
Before posting anything, run a quick mental checklist: Is it factual? Can I prove it? Does it imply superiority or guarantee outcomes? If yes to the last two, reword it.
Q6: When can you solicit testimonials or reviews?
✅ Correct: D — None of the time
Soliciting testimonials is not permitted regardless of consent. (Principle 1.10) The power dynamic means patients may feel obligated to leave positive reviews. Voluntarily left reviews are acceptable; soliciting them is not.
In Your Practice
Do not ask patients for Google reviews or send follow-up messages requesting feedback. Let reviews come organically.

✅ Answer: Principle 2 — Conflicts of Interest

Professional Boundaries — Principle 1: Maintain Professional & Personal Boundaries

›
Q1: Which of the following could be considered boundary crossings? Select all that apply.
✅ Correct: A, B, C
A — Sharing personal relationship struggles with a patient → Makes patient your emotional support
B — Accepting a free ticket to a social event from a patient → Creates personal relationship outside therapy
C — Encouraging more appointments to fill your schedule → Prioritizes business over patient needs
D is fine — Homecare instructions via email with consent is appropriate clinical communication ✅
In Your Practice
Don't share personal info with patients. Only have them book appointments they need. Don't attend social events hosted by patients.
Q2: True or False — The therapeutic relationship must be terminated if a boundary crossing has taken place.
✅ False
A boundary crossing doesn't automatically mean termination. You must acknowledge it, re-establish the boundary, and document it. Termination is only required for severe, repeated, or escalating cases (e.g. sexual misconduct).
In Your Practice
If a crossing occurs, gently redirect future conversations to clinical topics and document that you recognized and addressed it.
Q3: Ways to maintain professional boundaries through communication? Select all that apply.
✅ Correct: A, C
A — Observing and responding to non-verbal cues ✅
C — Interacting in a culturally respectful manner ✅
B — Using jargon creates barriers, not boundaries ❌
D — Never dismiss patient concerns ❌
In Your Practice
Speak kindly and with words patients can understand.
Q4: A patient says you remind them of their child. What is occurring?
✅ D — Transference
Transference is when a patient projects feelings from another relationship onto the practitioner. Counter-transference (A) is the opposite — when the practitioner projects their own feelings onto the patient.
In Your Practice
If a patient seems unusually attached or overly familiar, maintain professional boundaries and document if it becomes significant.

Professional Boundaries — Principle 2: Conflicts of Interest

›
Q1: True or False — Potential conflicts of interest are just as important to address as actual conflicts of interest.
✅ True
Potential conflicts are just as important as actual ones — you must identify and disclose them to patients either way, because even the perception of a conflict can undermine trust. (Principle 2.1)
In Your Practice
Keep things professional first and foremost. Speak directly with the patient about the potential conflict. Document those conversations and their outcomes in the patient record.
Q2: You've identified an actual conflict of interest. What must you document in the patient's health care record?
✅ Standard Answer (Principle 2.3)
Document the details and management of the conflict of interest — the conflict itself, the date it was discussed, the management options explored, and the decision/agreement reached.
In Your Practice
Document the conflict, the date it was discussed, the management options explored, and the decision/agreement reached.
Q3: You've been offered a gift from an organization that sells and markets products. What must you evaluate?
✅ Standard Answer (Principle 2.5)
Evaluate whether the gift could reasonably be perceived to influence patient care or your professional judgment — including whether it creates pressure to recommend or sell their products.
In Your Practice
Ask: will this pressure me to sell their product? Does it create a conflict of interest? If yes to either, decline.

Professional Boundaries — Principle 3: Dual Relationships

›
Q1: Fill in the blank — Dual relationships: when licensees have a _____ or _____ relationship with a patient outside of their practice.
✅ C — Business or Personal
Dual relationships: when licensees have a business or personal relationship with a patient outside of their practice.
In Your Practice
Living in a small town means many overlaps with people known outside of work. All have been offered other RMT options and choose to stay. Relationships are made professional only within the borders of treatment time.
Q2: You are considering treating a close friend. What does Principle 3 require you to consider?
✅ Standard Answer
Consider whether you can remain objective and unbiased in their treatment, whether the patient feels comfortable, and whether other RMT options are available to them.
In Your Practice
Evaluate objectivity, patient comfort, and availability of alternative practitioners before proceeding.
Q3: You must treat someone with whom you have a personal relationship. What are the four requirements?
✅ Standard Answer
(a) Assess whether you can remain objective in your clinical judgment
(b) Clearly communicate the dual relationship and any potential conflicts of interest to the patient
(c) Offer the option to seek care from another practitioner
(d) Maintain professional boundaries throughout treatment
In Your Practice
Assess objectivity, be transparent with the patient about the relationship, offer alternatives, and maintain professionalism throughout.

Professional Boundaries — Principle 4: Prevention of Sexual Misconduct & Abuse

›
Q1: You unintentionally make physical contact with a potentially sexualized area of a patient's body. What three things must you do?
✅ Standard Answer
1. Stop immediately
2. Inform the patient and apologize
3. Document the incident in the patient's health care record
In Your Practice
Stop, let them know and apologize, remain professional, and document it.
Q2: You have reasonable grounds to believe another practitioner has engaged in sexual misconduct. What is your responsibility?
✅ C — Comply with mandatory reporting requirements
You have a legal duty to report. You cannot wait for the patient to come forward or try to handle it yourself. The HPOA has mandatory reporting obligations for sexual misconduct.
Q3: True or False — Individual levels of comfort with disrobing should be accommodated.
✅ True
Always accommodate individual comfort levels with disrobing — offer alternatives, adjust draping, and never make a patient feel pressured.
Q4: Which requirement under Principle 4 relates to ensuring clear physical boundaries are maintained using visual barriers?
✅ Standard Answer — Appropriate Draping
Provide appropriate draping at all times, exposing only the area being treated. The drape acts as the visual barrier that maintains clear physical boundaries throughout treatment.
In Your Practice
Only one area uncovered at a time — everything else remains draped throughout the treatment.
Q5: True or False — Sexual relationships with current patients are always prohibited.
✅ True
Sexual relationships with current patients are always prohibited — no exceptions. The power imbalance in the therapeutic relationship means consent cannot be considered truly free.
Q6: You are considering entering a sexual relationship with a former patient. What must you consider?
✅ Standard Answer
Consider the nature and length of the prior therapeutic relationship, how much time has passed since it ended, whether a power imbalance still exists, and whether the relationship could be perceived as an exploitation of the therapeutic relationship.

Person-Centred Care — Principle 1: Provide Person-Centred Care

›
Q1: What must you do to provide person-centred care? Select all that apply.
✅ A, C, D
A — Collaborate with the patient in the decision-making process
C — Use active listening when addressing concerns or questions
D — Incorporate patients' care preferences and values into treatment

B is incorrect — you must inform the patient and take corrective action promptly if an adverse event occurs.
In Your Practice
Always explain the plan and check that it works for the patient. Incorporate their preferences and document appropriately.
Q2: A treatment has not proceeded as planned and resulted in an adverse event. What are you required to do?
✅ Standard Answer
1. Inform the patient of the adverse event
2. Take corrective action and provide appropriate assistance
3. Document it in the patient's health care record
In Your Practice
Inform the patient, provide assistance, and document immediately.
Q3: You provide your preferred treatment plan without presenting alternatives.
(a) Have you respected the patient's right to make informed choices?
(b) Have you prioritized the patient's health and well-being?
✅ Both No
(a) No — patients must be given alternatives and their right to make informed choices must be upheld (Principle 1.2)
(b) No — presenting only your preferred plan prioritizes your judgment over the patient's health and well-being (Principle 1.3)
Q4: Communication about treatment options and potential outcomes must meet which criteria? Select all that apply.
✅ A, B, D, E
A — Understandable · B — Complete · D — Current · E — Accurate
C (Positive) and F (Entertaining) are not requirements.
In Your Practice
Keep communication clear, jargon-free, and tailored to each patient's level of understanding.

Person-Centred Care — Principle 2: Continuity of Care

›
Q1: In addition to written notification, what other steps must be taken to terminate a therapeutic relationship?
✅ Standard Answer (Principle 2.3)
1. Document reasons for termination including steps taken to resolve the situation
2. Inform the patient they're entitled to a copy of their records and how to obtain them
3. Transfer records in a timely manner if requested
4. Provide a reasonable timeframe to find another practitioner and arrange interim care
5. Support them in finding another practitioner — unless termination was due to harassment, sexual conduct, discrimination, or risk of harm
Q2: A patient has acted in an aggressive and verbally abusive manner. True or False — You can terminate the therapeutic relationship immediately.
✅ True (Principle 2.1.4)
If a patient harasses, abuses, threatens, or engages in behaviour that places you at risk of harm, you can terminate immediately without providing prior notice.
In Your Practice — What to Document
Date of the incident, date of termination, reason for termination, and steps taken to try to resolve the situation beforehand.
Q3: Which of the following is NOT an acceptable reason to terminate a therapeutic relationship?
✅ C — Patient requires more time due to mobility differences
You cannot terminate a therapeutic relationship on discriminatory grounds. A patient needing more time due to a mobility difference is not an acceptable reason. (Principle 2.2)
Q4: You are closing your practice permanently. True or False — You are not required to arrange ongoing care for patients.
✅ False (Principle 2.4)
You must take reasonable steps to arrange ongoing care for patients when closing your practice permanently.
In Your Practice
Would provide referrals and transfer records to ensure patients have continuity of care.

Person-Centred Care — Principle 3: Collaborative Care

›
Q1: When can you share patient and treatment information with another health care practitioner?
✅ Standard Answer (Principle 3.3)
Only share relevant patient and treatment information where appropriate and with the patient's written consent.
In Your Practice
Always obtain patient consent before sharing any information with another practitioner.
Q2: True or False — You must collaborate with other practitioners by contributing knowledge, skills, and clinical experience to improve your professional reputation.
✅ False (Principle 3.1)
You must collaborate to improve patient outcomes — not your professional reputation.
In Your Practice
Recognize and respect the skills and roles of others involved in a patient's care. Collaboration is always patient-first.
Q3: True or False — Making appropriate referrals supports collaborative care.
✅ True (Principle 3.2)
Making appropriate referrals is part of collaborative, person-centred care. If a patient's needs fall outside your scope of practice, referring them to the right practitioner is the professional and ethical thing to do.
In Your Practice
Refer when needs are beyond scope — patient outcomes always come first.
